# Conversation director — phase 3

New browser promos explicitly activate the stateful director. Saved v5 duels without the director continue unchanged; the underlying graph and match payoff contract are retained. Headless legacy callers remain compatible. The current live audit opts in by default; run PROMO_AUDIT_LEGACY=1 node tests/promo-quality-baseline.test.cjs to reproduce the original scene shape.

The director snapshots both speaking identities (including a mouthpiece when present) and tracks the opening subject, active claim, latest actual spoken lines, turn history, bounded composure and irritation. Authored pressure/delivery wording maps to provisional reserved, combative, analytical or adaptive stances. This is a deterministic first-pass heuristic, not a complete emotional model; individual trigger matching and character-specific substantive writing are later work.

After the opening and answer, a settled, lopsided or uncomfortable exchange may go directly to the final word. A heated or unresolved exchange can take one extra beat. Recorded disputed evidence retains the existing firm/revise/leave options, and a recorded commitment retains a follow-up opportunity. Other follow-ups can press the subject, acknowledge the reply, hold silence, or leave it there. Acknowledgement and silence do not receive the same automatic heat bonus as pressing. Every scene still reaches the graph's closing decision and its existing promise/tactic semantics.

The browser removes fixed round numbering for directed scenes and shows subject and concise speaker moods in the existing panel. No save migration, new CI workflow, external model or arbitrary finish change. Three or four decisions are deliberately bounded for this phase; genuinely different formats and extended multi-turn dialogue follow in phases 4–6. The new test exercises both paths, all new follow-ups, exact save/resume, promise preservation, mouthpiece identity and a disputed-case routing guard. Persistent semantic memory beyond the active scene is deferred to phase 7.

The audit distinguishes the current directed path from the historical four-round baseline. Exact text overlap remains a proxy; new structure is not proof of improved writing.

## Phase 6A — spontaneous reactions

New v2 director snapshots allow one deterministic reaction after the answer, based on the opponent's authored pressure stance, composure, irritation, the chosen action and the room meter. An interruption of the interviewer's handover creates an extra opportunity to respond; a refusal of another question or a silence leads to the final choice. These are stage directions, not invented spoken claims. The existing graph still owns evidence and the final promise; the event cannot fire in an active rivalry case, programme-directed segment or mouthpiece scene. Saved v1 scenes keep their old pacing. No new workflows or runtime model. This is a bounded first tranche, not the full phase: actual mid-speech cutoffs, player-initiated walkouts, wider scene lengths and richer improvisation are deferred.

The semantic layer had a single opponent counter and press-counter line per topic and a hard voice preference for multi-frame pools. New formatted semantic scenes now use a second manually written alternative for each of the 39 topics (78 lines), plus neutral alternatives for unclassified questions, settlements and acknowledgement. Stable frame indices and prior-save selection are retained. Seeded selection uses the existing preference on a minority of seeds instead of locking every wrestler into the same preferred wording. All alternatives preserve the claim and reply-to provenance; protected recorded cases are still excluded. Tests check reachability and claim preservation per topic/move and replay the 960-scene numerical/contract comparison with the old wording policy. This addresses the semantic subset, not repeated protected graph replies or the full character-voice problem.

## Repetition pass D1 — bank selection and coverage

The semantic route deliberately bypassed authored banks for every direct rebuttal, press and acknowledgement. Restoring arbitrary old turn/press wording would reintroduce false claims. The first safe restoration is exact-text-reviewed neutral acknowledgements: 36 player lines and 33 opponent settlement lines. The bank selector still enforces the owner, slot, conditions, protected arguments and recent-ID exclusion. Wider exact-variant coverage, substantive authored rebuttals and closing-contract review are separate work. No new workflows.

## Repetition pass F — failed delivery

The original three comic mishaps per voice often claimed a sentence was forgotten or a microphone accident occurred even though the entire spoken line was in the transcript. New formatted scenes instead use 48 voice-specific and 21 pressure/closing delivery notes. They describe cadence, timing, projection, hesitation and emphasis without asserting missing words or invented incidents. Composure, irritation and mic skill influence the eligible pool; final statements have their own pool. Notes name the actual speaker (including mouthpieces), avoid repeats within the scene and prefer unused recent history wording. The existing failure roll, full spoken line, crowd reaction, number of notes, graph, promises and score are unchanged. Old saves without deliveryStyle retain the original FLUFF policy. Tests compare the same 240 scenes with the old policy and require identical non-fluff dialogue, graph, consequences, decision beats and conversation state. This pass does not add literal mid-speech interruptions, new failure rolls or directed follow-up failure events; those are separate work. No new workflows.

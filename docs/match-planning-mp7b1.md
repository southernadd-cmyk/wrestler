# MP7B.1 — sequence preference calibration

MP7B audited 79 proposals, all two moves, zero control changes.
The problem was selection pressure and pruning, not absence of
validated graph chains. MP7A found 120 control-changing sequences
across its 1,920 shortlisted candidates.

For new rooms only, opt in to policy 2. Reserve up to eight of 24
beam entries for actual reversal paths and retain one two-performer
chain per length if available. Keep the original 240/192 work limits
and six-candidate shortlist. No fabricated transitions or waived
safety checks. The previous policy remains the default for legacy
callers and saved rooms.

A shared Sugeno model estimates complexity and exchange preference
from the plan, weaker ring ability, both workers' cooperation and
flexibility, and greater caution. These are soft preferences, not
new permissions. Rank legal candidates by fit to desired length,
both workers' fuzzy assessment, control exchange and risk; scale
the old merit contribution to avoid over-penalizing length.
Safe plans, injured or severely fatigued participants receive only
two-move non-risky candidates, or no offer. No random diversity quotas.

An 80-room paired audit compares the original and revised live
policies, reports lengths, genuine two-performer chains, per-plan
distribution, stance distribution, unique paths and cautious offers.
It also rechecks bans, fixed work bounds, frozen reload, actual mood
and signed named-call parity. A selected setup remains provisional;
MP7C persists conditional commitments. All calibration results
remain pending CI.

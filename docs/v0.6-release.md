# The Office — v0.6.0 release guide

**Release date:** 21 November 2026  
**Edition:** *Earn the finish*  
**Code scope:** Match-plan realism and endurance work delivered after v0.5.0, including the completed style audits and the final earned-recovery improvement. No new career or save format.

> The matches are scripted. The people aren't.

## What changes for players

The five match plans now have better-defined identities, and their major moments have to be earned by something that *actually happened* in the ring.

- **Technical:** held grips, linked takedowns, counters, and legal follow-throughs distinguish trained workers without allowing impossible transitions.
- **Brawl:** real running attacks, hard exchanges and named late impacts come from plausible rope-run and position setups. In long bouts, conditioned Brawlers can earn a limited pacing window through a performed in-ring breather or grounded hold.
- **Spot-fest:** trained flyers build toward real top-rope and springboard attempts, with a chance of counters or botches. In 15+ minute matches, competent but not elite Spectacle workers who are too tired for a big leap can favour affordable named lucha, strike and neck offence that is already legally selectable.
- **Story:** hope spots and defensive reversals need actual actions, with follow-through earned by the match rather than narration alone.
- **Safe:** excludes the most hazardous **scripted** high spots, including extreme floor/balcony spectacle, but **does not ban normal legal wrestling**. One ordinary scripted diving elbow remains available only with the requisite ringcraft, safety, stamina and legitimate top-rope preparation.

### A near fall must have a source

A scheduled pin attempt does not create a fictional high-impact move. The engine checks a genuinely landed, suitably recent, owner-correct mat knockdown before a cover can be credited, and that source cannot be used again. The booked winner and the physical legality of ordinary moves remain intact. The five plans are not flattened into one style to achieve this.

### Long matches are still demanding

The 21 November change is a **bounded improvement**, not universal fatigue removal. A genuine rest costs a performed action; low-cost named finishing attacks must still pass the normal graph, position, stamina and attempt rules. No stamina is awarded by merely inspecting a move, and no stars are granted for selecting a preferred action.

## Evidence and QA

- [Long-match endurance and late offence — change and matched evidence](long-match-endurance-and-late-offence.md), merged via [PR #158](https://github.com/southernadd-cmyk/wrestler/pull/158): 1,440 baseline and 1,440 updated real OfficeMatch bouts across five plans, 8/12/18 minutes, three synthetic skill tiers and four opponent fixtures. All 1,248 unaffected tapes were identical. Every booked finish and near-fall source remained valid.
- [Earned near-fall source/finish protection](https://github.com/southernadd-cmyk/wrestler/pull/154) and [Safe scripted spectacle boundary](https://github.com/southernadd-cmyk/wrestler/pull/156) were separately tested and merged.
- [Graph Lab](../tools/graph-lab/) retains the read-only, seeded comparison fixtures and real event tapes.
- [Five-plan quality/realism audit, PR #157](https://github.com/southernadd-cmyk/wrestler/pull/157) records the remaining fatigue, late offence and commentary-repetition concerns; that read-only audit PR is separate from the v0.6 match-code release.
- The combined PR #158 change passed seven existing GitHub workflows before merge; it did not add a new CI workflow.

**Representative 18-minute results from PR #158's matched A/B tests:** overall Brawl last-three-minute distinct named actions averaged **1.19 → 1.31** per match, and Spot-fest **0.83 → 0.94**. The change is deliberately narrow, so broad-group averages move less than the directly eligible workers. Exhaustion remains significant, especially for inexperienced performers. The results describe simulated bouts, not real-world injury safety.

## Commentary follow-up — v0.6.1

The post-v0.6.0 booth-memory pass in [PR #160](https://github.com/southernadd-cmyk/wrestler/pull/160) already counted **successful, non-reversed moves from the actual event ledger**, added Vic's tactical pattern recognition, protected submission-only idioms, and corrected Ravi's road-agent voice. Across the reported selected 240 paired fights it cut exact sentences repeated three or more times from 332 to 79; that sample does **not** prove repetition has disappeared.

The v0.6.1 follow-up replaces Gloria's automatic count announcements after every repeated move with a third-use milestone, alternate action descriptions and sparse, event-backed observations. Vic distinguishes the **first and second actual counters** after a move has repeatedly landed. More named graph techniques get clear labels; other authored moves still use their own graph names. The booth now marks narrated graph lines with an optional action ID for exact residual-phrase attribution; the physical event ledger and match calculations remain authoritative.

A focused regression fixture covers the previously reported Story seed `762728`, all five plans, three match lengths, submission-only wording, and a reproducible per-action breakdown of remaining repetition. A paired comparison against the pre-patch engine can assert event tape, outcome, rating, stamina, botches, injuries and near-fall identity without adding any new workflow. Remaining semantic loops in resets, setups and other non-impact texture are a **further editorial audit**, not a claim this patch eliminates all duplicate narration.

## Compatibility and limits

The release makes no new save-file requirements; an existing browser career remains usable. It does not add wrestler biographies, change the booking contracts, award guaranteed move landings, or modify the existing Graph Lab read-only gameplay.

**Still to improve:** 18-minute pacing for less-skilled wrestlers; residual repetitive commentary, especially generic resets and non-impact setup texture; and further human review of whether final sequences feel plausible. Star-rating parity across match plans is not the goal.
